Telecommunications, Signal Processing & Machine Learning

Jonathan Nogueira Gois

Control and Automation Engineering, CEFET-RJ – Nova Iguaçu Postdoctoral Researcher, Universidade Federal do Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ)

Jonathan Nogueira Gois was born in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, in 1990. He graduated in Electronic and Computer Engineering from the Universidade Federal do Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ) in 2013, and received his M.Sc. in Electrical Engineering in 2016 from the Electrical Engineering Program (PEE) of COPPE/UFRJ, working on multiresolution decompositions for infrared–visible video fusion. He earned his Ph.D. in 2025 from the Graduate Program in Electrical and Telecommunications Engineering (PPGEET) at the Universidade Federal Fluminense (UFF), with a dissertation on machine learning applied to mobile and optical communication systems.

He joined CEFET-RJ as a Professor in 2016, teaching Electrical Engineering at the Nova Friburgo campus until 2024. Since 2024 he has been a Professor in the Control and Automation Engineering program at the Nova Iguaçu campus, and since 2025 he has also been a postdoctoral researcher in Telecommunications and Signal Processing at UFRJ.

His current research addresses distributed max–min power allocation and dynamic cooperation clustering for cell-free massive MIMO networks with multiple central processing units, developed within the Brasil 6G initiative, and he takes part in a FAPERJ project on microwave-photonic hybrid access networks. Earlier work includes machine-learning models for short-range optical fiber links (SI-POF/OFDM), antenna selection for massive MIMO, and computer-vision methods for infrared–visible video fusion and automatic sign-language recognition.

Research

  • Cell-Free Massive MIMO & Multi-CPU Networks
  • Distributed Resource Allocation
  • Microwave Photonics & Hybrid Optical-Wireless Access
  • Machine Learning for Communications
  • Antenna Selection for Massive MIMO
  • Signal & Image Processing
  • Brasil 6G — Distributed Power Allocation for Cell-Free Massive MIMO Multi-CPU Networks Fixed-point power balancing (DFPB) and dynamic cooperation clustering across cooperating CPUs, benchmarked against centralized max–min and prior distributed baselines.
  • FAPERJ MWP-NET — Microwave Photonics for Next-Generation Access Networks Hybrid WDM-FTTx and 5G/6G wireless access, joint optical–RF frequency conversion, and atmospheric spectroscopic sensing over the same photonic front end.
  • CNPq Universal — Statistical Learning for Telecommunications Statistical and machine learning methods applied to enable next-generation mobile communications, with T. N. Ferreira (coordinator).
  • Machine Learning for SI-POF Optical Links Data-driven models (gradient boosting, DMT/OFDM signal characterization) for step-index polymer optical fiber transmission — doctoral research at UFF.
  • Antenna Selection & Power Allocation for Massive MIMO Low-complexity matching-pursuit algorithms for antenna subset selection under sum-rate and channel-hardening objectives.
